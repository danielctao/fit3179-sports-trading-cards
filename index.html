Australia's Sports Trading Cards

Exploring how Australian sports trading-card collections have evolved, who gets represented, and how the geography of Australian football and rugby league shapes the sporting landscape.

01 · The Sporting Landscape

Two codes, one country

Australian football and rugby league occupy very different parts of Australia's sporting landscape. Compare participation across the states and territories, then switch between adults and children to see how the pattern changes by generation.

How to read the map: Blue states have higher Australian football participation, while orange states have higher rugby league participation. The stronger the colour, the larger the percentage-point difference between the two codes.

Source: Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay, January–December 2025. Released April 2026.

02 · The Collection Evolves

More than just a base set

AFL Footy Stars has developed into an increasingly complex trading-card collection. Across the four releases examined, the number of distinct card categories has generally increased.

What stands out: The number of card categories increased from 13 in 2020 to 26 in 2026. Although the 2024 release recorded a small decline, the overall collection became substantially more diverse across the period.

Source: Select Australia, AFL Footy Stars official checklists, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026.

What changed inside the collection?

The growth in card categories was not evenly distributed. Some category families expanded substantially, while others remained stable or disappeared across releases.

What stands out: Parallel and variant cards expanded considerably by 2026, while signature and patch formats became far more prominent. The heatmap reveals that the growth seen in the previous chart came from changes in the composition of the collection, not simply the addition of more base cards.

Category families are analytical groupings created from the official checklist category names. Cell values represent the number of distinct categories in each family.

03 · Inside a Modern Collection

A modern set is far more than its base cards

The 2026 AFL Footy Stars collection combines base cards with character cards, player features, parallels, premium inserts, signatures and patches. The hierarchy below shows how these different parts fit together.

How to read the chart: The inner ring represents broad category families, while the outer ring breaks each family into individual checklist categories. Larger arcs contain more reported card designs.

Method: Category families use the same analytical grouping introduced in the previous heatmap. Arc size represents the number of distinct card designs reported in the official checklist, not the number of physical cards produced.

Source: Select Australia, 2026 AFL Footy Stars official checklist.

How did rarity become part of the collection?

The collection did not only grow in size. Across the releases examined, many categories were associated with explicit scarcity signals such as serial numbering and stated pack odds.

What stands out: Serial numbering is strongly concentrated in the collection's premium category families. Parallel and variant cards contribute 13 serial-numbered categories, while all 10 signature and patch categories are serial-numbered.

Method: Each flow represents distinct checklist categories. Categories are classified as Serial-numbered when a serial limit is stated; Pack odds only when odds are stated without a serial limit; and Neither stated when neither scarcity signal appears in the checklist.

04 · Who Gets Represented

Some positions get more of the spotlight

Not every AFL player appears across the same number of trading-card designs. Compare the distribution of card representation across player positions and switch between releases to see how the pattern changes over time.

What stands out: In 2026, midfielders and midfielder/forwards typically appeared across more card designs per player than defenders, forwards and rucks. The spread also shows that a small number of players receive considerably more representation than others.

Method: Each point represents one uniquely matched AFL player in the selected release. Card designs are counted from checklist entries associated with that player. AFLW cards, non-player subjects and ambiguous player matches are excluded.

Source: Select Australia AFL Footy Stars official checklists, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026; player-position data from AFLPlayerStats.

05 · Participation at Scale

Where are the largest participation pools?

Participation rates reveal which code is relatively stronger, but they do not show the absolute size of the participant base. This map compares estimated participant numbers across Australia's states and territories.

How to read the map: Larger circles represent more estimated participants. Use the selectors to switch between Australian football and rugby league, and between adults and children.

Method: Symbols are positioned at the geographic centroid of each state or territory. Circle area represents the estimated number of participants reported by AusPlay.

Source: Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay, January–December 2025. Released April 2026.

What happens when participation reshapes the map?

A traditional map gives every state its real geographic area, regardless of how many people participate in the sport. These cartograms instead resize each state according to its estimated participant population.

How to read the cartogram: Each circle represents a state or territory, positioned roughly according to its geographic location. Larger circles indicate more estimated participants. The same size scale is used for both sports so the two panels can be compared directly.

Method: This is a Dorling-style cartogram. State and territory shapes are replaced with circles and arranged schematically to preserve the broad geography of Australia. Circle area represents estimated participants rather than geographic land area.

Source: Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay, January–December 2025. Released April 2026.

Australia's Sports Trading Cards

Exploring how Australian sports trading card collections have evolved, who gets represented, and how the geography of Australian football and rugby league shapes the sporting landscape.

01 · The Sporting Landscape

Australian Football vs Rugby League

Australian football and rugby league dominate different parts of Australia's sporting landscape. Compare participation across states and territories, then switch between adults and children.

How to read the map: Dark teal states have higher Australian football participation, while light green states have higher rugby league participation. Stronger differences are shown toward the ends of the colour scale.

02 · The Collection Evolves

More than just a base set

AFL Footy Stars has become increasingly complex. Across four releases, the number of distinct card categories has generally increased.

What drove the 2026 peak: Parallel / Variant and Signature / Patch categories accounted for 17 of the 26 categories or about 65% of the 2026 collection. In 2020, these two families accounted for only 2 categories combined.

What changed inside the collection?

The growth in card categories was not evenly distributed. Some category families expanded substantially, while others remained stable or disappeared across releases.

What stands out: Parallel / Variant categories expanded from 2 in 2020 to 6 in 2022 and reached 9 by 2026. Signature / Patch categories also grew sharly, from none in 2020 to 8 in 2026. These highlighted cells show where some of the strongest structural changes occured in the collection.

Category families are analytical groupings created from the official checklist category names. Cell values represent the number of distinct categories in each family.

03 · Inside a Modern Collection

Inside the 2026 collection

The 2026 Footy Stars collection extends far beyond its base cards. Character cards, parallels, premium inserts, signatures and patches create a much deeper collection structure.

How to read the chart: The inner ring represents broad category families, while the outer ring breaks each family into individual checklist categories. Larger arcs contain more reported card designs.

Method: Category families use the same analytical grouping introduced in the previous heatmap. Arc size represents the number of distinct card designs reported in the official checklist, not the number of physical cards produced.

How did rarity become part of the collection?

The collection did not only grow in size. Across the releases examined, many categories were associated with explicit scarcity signals such as serial numbering and stated pack odds.

What stands out: Serial numbering is strongly concentrated in the collection's premium category families. Parallel and variant cards contribute 13 serial-numbered categories, while all 10 signature and patch categories are serial-numbered.

Method: Each flow represents distinct checklist categories. Categories are classified as Serial-numbered when a serial limit is stated; Pack odds only when odds are stated without a serial limit; and Neither stated when neither scarcity signal appears in the checklist.

04 · Who Gets Represented

How much representation does each position receive?

Not every AFL player receives the same level of trading-card representation. Compare card appearances across player positions and see how the pattern changes over time.

How to read the chart: The dot shows the median number of card designs per player. The thicker line covers the middle 50% of players, while the thin line shows the full observed range.

Method: Player-level card representation is summarised by AFL position for the selected release. The thick interval represents the middle 50% of players, the dot represents the median and the thin line shows the full observed range. AFLW cards, non-player subjects and ambiguous player matches are excluded.

Where does player representation appear?

Player positions are not represented evenly across every type of trading card. This chord diagram connects AFL player positions with the card-category families in which those players appeared in the 2026 Footy Stars collection.

How to read the chord diagram: Wider ribbons represent more matched card designs connecting a player position to a category family. Ribbon colour follows the category family. Hover over a ribbon or outer arc to view the exact number of card designs.

What stands out: Parallel and variant cards dominate player representation in the 2026 collection, accounting for 1,033 matched card designs. Midfielders have the largest overall representation, with their strongest connection occurring in the Parallel / Variant family.

Method: The diagram includes player-linked 2026 AFL Footy Stars checklist entries with a usable player-position match. Non-player subjects, AFLW cards and unresolved player matches are excluded.

05 · Participation at Scale

Where are Australia's largest participation pools?

Participation rates show where each sport is relatively stronger. Participant counts reveal something different: the actual size of each state's sporting population.

How to read the map: Larger circles represent more estimated participants. Use the selectors to switch between Australian football and rugby league, and between adults and children.

Method: Symbols are positioned at the geographic centroid of each state or territory. Circle area represents the estimated number of participants reported by AusPlay.

What happens when participation reshapes the map?

A conventional map gives every state its real geographic area, regardless of how many people participate in the sport. These contiguous cartograms resize the states according to estimated participation while preserving their neighbouring relationships.

How to read the cartogram: Each state or territory is resized according to its estimated participant population. Larger regions represent more participants, while darker colours indicate higher participation rates. Hover over a region for exact values.

06 · Comparing the Collections

How differently are the two collections built?

AFL Footy Stars and NRL League Heroes structure their 2026 collections differently. Comparing category-family shares reveals where each collection places its emphasis.

How to read the chart: Each row represents a category family. Blue represents Australian football and red represents rugby league. Wider gaps show larger differences between the two collections.

What stands out: The strongest differences are in Signature / Patch, which makes up 30.8% of Australian football categories but 0% of rugby league, and Predictor / Redemption, which makes up 27.8% of rugby league categories but 0% of Australian football.

Method: Category families use the same analytical grouping applied throughout the previous collection visualisations. Shares are calculated within each 2026 collection.

How did the two collections evolve?

The 2026 collections look very different, but those differences developed over time. Compare how the share of category families changed between 2020 and 2026 for each sport.

What stands out: From 2020 to 2026, Australian football’s largest increases were Signature / Patch and Parallel / Variant. In the available rugby league 2020 data, Player Feature / Performance fell sharply while Predictor / Redemption and Premium / Limited emerged by 2026.

Method: Category-family shares are calculated from the number of distinct categories within each available 2020 and 2026 collection. The same analytical family classification used throughout the previous visualisations is applied here.
